# Custom Slack App vs Buying Another Tool: When to Build vs Buy

A framework for deciding when to build a custom Slack app instead of buying another SaaS tool. Learn when custom Slack apps, workflows, and automation make sense for fintech and ecommerce teams.

**Published:** July 8, 2026

---

## Buy a Tool When:

- The workflow is standard — payroll, basic CRM, invoicing
- You need it live this week, not next month
- The process isn't part of your actual differentiation

## Build a Custom Slack App When:

### 1. Your workflow IS the differentiator

A fintech client had a fraud-review process that didn't map onto any off-the-shelf platform without painful workarounds. We built a Slack app instead: flagged transactions post into a review channel with approve/deny buttons and a full audit trail. No context-switching, no second login, no lag between detection and decision.

### 2. You're paying full price for a fraction of the product

If your team touches 20% of the features on a $40K/year platform, that's overhead disguised as infrastructure. A scoped app covering just those workflows is usually cheaper to build AND maintain than the license, onboarding, and admin overhead combined.

### 3. Decisions need to happen where the data already lives

For an ecommerce brand, order exceptions — payment failures, inventory mismatches — used to get buried in a dashboard nobody opened until it was too late. We moved that logic into Slack: alerts fire where ops already works, with one-click actions to resolve on the spot.

### 4. You're scaling a manual process that breaks with headcount

If someone on your team is the "integration layer" between two disconnected tools — copy-pasting, cross-checking, manually triggering the next step — that's a build signal, not a hiring signal. When a manual process is eating hours every week, a custom Slack app eliminates the bottleneck entirely.

## The real question isn't "build vs. buy"

It's: does this workflow touch how you make money or serve customers in a way that's genuinely specific to you?

If yes, a generic tool will always be a compromise. If no, buy it and move on.
